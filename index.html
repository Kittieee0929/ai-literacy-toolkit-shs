01 / START HERE

Use the toolkit like a learner,
not like an answer machine.

This website is a developmental research toolkit for Senior High School learners. Work through the seven domains, complete the activities, and use the reusable tools to practice responsible AI-supported learning.

RESEARCH STATUS Needs-based learning resource informed by the completed assessment and evaluated by experts and students.

The study included 239 needs-assessment responses (overall M = 2.982: High Learning Support Needed), three expert evaluations (M = 3.760: Highly Valid), and 262 student acceptability responses (M = 3.875: Highly Acceptable). Source Verification (M = 3.072) received the strongest needs-based emphasis. These evaluations do not establish measurable learning gains.

STUDENT SAFETY RULES

Verify important claims. AI can sound confident and still be wrong.

Protect personal data. Do not paste unnecessary private or confidential information.

Follow task rules. Teacher and school instructions still matter when AI is used.

Keep human judgment. AI should support your thinking, not replace responsibility.

05 / ABOUT THE STUDY

Designed for students.
Informed by their needs.

This seven-domain AI Literacy Toolkit was developed for Grade 11–12 learners in participating Laoag City schools, guided by the needs assessment (n = 239). Source Verification ranked highest (M = 3.072), while every domain was rated High Learning Support Needed. Expert validation (M = 3.760) and student acceptability (M = 3.875) supported its suitability as a supplementary learning resource.

The research is developmental in scope. It does not test whether the toolkit improves grades, performance, or long-term outcomes. The study included needs assessment → toolkit design and development → expert validation → student acceptability evaluation → recommendations for continued refinement.

Needs assessment→ Develop→ Expert validate→ Revise→ Student acceptability→ Refine
RESEARCH PROJECT Developing and Validating a Needs-Based AI Literacy Toolkit for Senior High School Students in Laoag City, Ilocos Norte
DESIGN Developmental research guided by ADDIE

Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation guide the toolkit development process.

PARTICIPANTS Grade 11–12 Senior High School learners

Selected learners from INNHS and INCAT in Laoag City, with expert validators participating in the validation process.

INSTRUMENTS Three researcher-made tools

AI Literacy Needs Assessment Questionnaire, Expert Validation Questionnaire, and Student Acceptability Questionnaire.

SCOPE Development and validation — not effectiveness testing

The study does not measure improvement in grades, AI-literacy performance, or long-term outcomes through experimental testing.

EXPERT VALIDATION Criteria rated by experts

Content quality · instructional value · usability · accessibility · visual design

STUDENT ACCEPTABILITY Criteria rated by students

Usability · clarity · relevance · usefulness · visual appeal

RESEARCH DATA ≠ TOOLKIT ACTIVITY DATA

Research data collection is separate from the learning features in this prototype and follows the required ethics, school-permission, consent/assent, and parental-consent procedures. Browser-only progress and draft responses are not presented as research responses.

06 / QUICK GLOSSARY

Words worth knowing
before you trust the tool.

Use these definitions while working through the seven domains.

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Computer systems designed to perform tasks such as recognizing patterns, making predictions, classifying information, or generating content.

Generative AI

AI that produces new outputs such as text, images, audio, code, or video based on patterns learned from data and the instructions it receives.

Prompt

The instruction, question, context, or input a user gives an AI system.

AI model

A computational system trained to identify patterns and produce predictions or outputs from input.

Hallucination

A confident-sounding AI output that contains fabricated, unsupported, or incorrect information.

Bias

A systematic tendency that can lead to unfair, incomplete, or distorted outputs or decisions.

Deepfake / synthetic media

AI-generated or manipulated media that can realistically imitate a person, voice, image, or event.

Personal data

Information that identifies a person directly or can reasonably be linked to them, including combinations of details.

Source verification

The process of locating evidence, checking credibility and context, and confirming that a source actually supports a claim.

Academic integrity

Honest and responsible academic work that follows task rules and accurately represents the learner’s own contribution.

Human agency

The ability and responsibility of people to set goals, make decisions, exercise judgment, and remain accountable when using AI.

AI literacy

The knowledge, skills, and judgment needed to understand, use, evaluate, and engage with AI responsibly and critically.

FINAL INTEGRATED CAPSTONE

One AI task. Seven decisions.

You are preparing a research presentation with AI. The chatbot gives a useful outline, one questionable statistic, a source you cannot locate, and suggests uploading a class spreadsheet. You also want to use an AI-generated image based on a classmate’s photo.

Select all seven domain cards before comparing your decision with a model response.
STRONG INTEGRATED RESPONSE

Keep useful brainstorming support, but evaluate the outline rather than accepting it automatically. Verify the questionable statistic and locate the original evidence for any source you plan to cite. Follow the task’s academic-integrity rules and make the final analysis, argument, and presentation your own. Do not upload identifiable class records when anonymized or synthetic data will do. Do not use a classmate’s likeness without considering consent, dignity, and possible harm. Use AI as a learning partner throughout: ask targeted questions, think through the response, check and verify what matters, improve your own work, and take responsibility for the final presentation.

AI Literacy Toolkit — Learner Quick Guide

Think first. Verify. Decide. Keep human judgment in control.

1. AI or Algorithm?Know what the system is doing, what it can do, and what still needs human judgment.
2. Six-Lens Output CheckCheck Accuracy, Relevance, Completeness, Logic, Bias, and Evidence.
3. VERIFYView source · Examine author · Review publisher · Inspect date/context · Find support · Your judgment.
4. AI Assistance SpectrumAsk whether AI is supporting learning, heavily contributing, or replacing the work being assessed.
5. Prompt Privacy ScannerRemove unnecessary names, IDs, contact details, records, and other personal information.
6. Ethics CourtAsk who is affected, what harm could occur, whether consent/ownership matters, and who is accountable.
7. AI WISE Learning RoutineASK → THINK → CHECK → VERIFY → IMPROVE → OWN.

Four habits to carry with you

Verify important claims. Fluent AI output can still be wrong.

Protect personal data. Share only what is necessary.

Follow academic rules. Teacher and school instructions still apply.

Keep human agency. AI can support decisions; it should not replace responsibility.
