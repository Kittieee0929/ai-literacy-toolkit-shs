STUDENT SAFETY RULES

Verify important claims. AI can sound confident and still be wrong.

Protect personal data. Do not paste unnecessary private or confidential information.

Follow task rules. Teacher and school instructions still matter when AI is used.

Keep human judgment. AI should support your thinking, not replace responsibility.

05 / ABOUT THE STUDY

Designed for students.
Built from identified needs.

This AI Literacy Toolkit is a needs-based educational resource being developed for Grade 11–12 learners in Laoag City, Ilocos Norte. Its seven domains align the study objectives with practical learner activities, reusable decision tools, reflection, and application.

The research is developmental in scope. It does not test whether the toolkit improves grades, performance, or long-term outcomes. The intended research sequence is needs assessment → toolkit development → expert validation → revision → student acceptability evaluation → final refinement.

Needs assessment→ Develop→ Expert validate→ Revise→ Student acceptability→ Refine
RESEARCH PROJECT Developing and Validating a Needs-Based AI Literacy Toolkit for Senior High School Students in Laoag City, Ilocos Norte
DESIGN Developmental research guided by ADDIE

Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation guide the toolkit development process.

PARTICIPANTS Grade 11–12 Senior High School learners

Selected learners from INNHS and INCAT in Laoag City, with expert validators participating in the validation process.

INSTRUMENTS Three researcher-made tools

AI Literacy Needs Assessment Questionnaire, Expert Validation Questionnaire, and Student Acceptability Questionnaire.

SCOPE Development and validation — not effectiveness testing

The study does not measure improvement in grades, AI-literacy performance, or long-term outcomes through experimental testing.

EXPERT VALIDATION What experts will examine

Content quality · instructional value · usability · accessibility · visual design

STUDENT ACCEPTABILITY What learners will evaluate

Usability · clarity · relevance · usefulness · visual appeal

RESEARCH DATA ≠ TOOLKIT ACTIVITY DATA

Research data collection is separate from the learning features in this prototype and follows the required ethics, school-permission, consent/assent, and parental-consent procedures. Browser-only progress and draft responses are not presented as research responses.

06 / QUICK GLOSSARY

Words worth knowing
before you trust the tool.

Use these definitions while working through the seven domains.

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Computer systems designed to perform tasks such as recognizing patterns, making predictions, classifying information, or generating content.

Generative AI

AI that produces new outputs such as text, images, audio, code, or video based on patterns learned from data and the instructions it receives.

Prompt

The instruction, question, context, or input a user gives an AI system.

AI model

A computational system trained to identify patterns and produce predictions or outputs from input.

Hallucination

A confident-sounding AI output that contains fabricated, unsupported, or incorrect information.

Bias

A systematic tendency that can lead to unfair, incomplete, or distorted outputs or decisions.

Deepfake / synthetic media

AI-generated or manipulated media that can realistically imitate a person, voice, image, or event.

Personal data

Information that identifies a person directly or can reasonably be linked to them, including combinations of details.

Source verification

The process of locating evidence, checking credibility and context, and confirming that a source actually supports a claim.

Academic integrity

Honest and responsible academic work that follows task rules and accurately represents the learner’s own contribution.

Human agency

The ability and responsibility of people to set goals, make decisions, exercise judgment, and remain accountable when using AI.

AI literacy

The knowledge, skills, and judgment needed to understand, use, evaluate, and engage with AI responsibly and critically.

Built from research,

These sources help ground the toolkit’s human-centered, critical, ethical, and educational approach to AI literacy.

UNESCO • 2024 AI Competency Framework for Students

Human-centered mindset, ethics of AI, AI techniques and applications, and AI system design across Understand, Apply, and Create levels.

 UNESCO • 2023 Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research

Human-centered, age-appropriate, privacy-aware, ethical, and pedagogically meaningful use of generative AI.

 UNESCO • 2024 AI Competency Framework for Teachers

Supports teacher knowledge, ethics, pedagogy, human agency, and professional learning in the AI era.

 Long & Magerko • 2020 What is AI Literacy?

A foundational competency-oriented framework for interacting with and critically evaluating AI.

 Ng et al. • 2021 Conceptualizing AI Literacy

Organizes AI literacy around knowing and understanding, using and applying, evaluating and creating, and ethical issues.

 Philippines • RA 10173 Data Privacy Act of 2012

Philippine legal context for protecting personal information in information and communications systems.

 DepEd • DO 003, s. 2026 Foundational Guidelines on AI in Basic Education

Frames AI in basic education as complementary, human-centered, inclusive, pedagogically appropriate, and protective of human agency and judgment.
